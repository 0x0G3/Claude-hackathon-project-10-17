Next Door · accessible phone · draft Oct 17

The promise

The same plan, easier to see, hear and tap.

Same flow, same safety rules, same checked facts. Turned on from the first screen, by the person or their care manager. It stays on for every screen and the printout.

Who it helps, and what changes

Low visionBody text 22 px, questions 34 px. Navy on white. Meaning never by color alone. Reading is hardOne short sentence per line. Every icon has a word. “Read it to me” on every screen. Shaky hands, a walkerButtons 68 px tall, 16 px apart. No swipes, no double taps, no long press. Hard of hearingNothing is sound-only. “Calls are hard” leads to going in person or staff calling with them. Stress, memoryOne thing per screen. “Step 1 of 3”. No timers. Nothing disappears on its own.
Bigger text Opened from the “Make it easier” button on the normal start screen. Grows with the phone’s own text setting, up to 200%.
Read it to me A speaker button at the top of every screen reads it out slowly, in the chosen language.
Simple steps The plan comes one step per screen, with Back and Next always in the same place.

Rules that change

  • No all-caps text. Caps are harder to read.
  • No grey text for anything that matters.
  • Small text never sits on sand or yellow.
  • Selected things say so in words: “Selected ✓”.
  • Focus ring: 3 px navy on white, yellow on navy.

Screen readers

  • One heading per screen; reading order matches what you see.
  • Buttons say what they do: “Call Yale Navigation Center”.
  • Icons are hidden from readers; the word next to them is read.
  • Each screen sets its language for the voice.
If they can’t hear the sound Turned on with “I can’t hear well” in Make it easier.
Words for every soundRead aloud always shows a “Now reading” caption. No beeps or alerts that are sound only.
No call requiredEvery plan offers “your care manager calls for you” or “go in person” before a phone number.
Relay and interpretersShow 7-1-1 relay next to every phone number. Offer to ask staff for a sign language interpreter.
Show, don’t say“What to say” becomes a card to show or type. Safety screens never rely on sound.
