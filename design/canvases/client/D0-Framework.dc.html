Who it’s for

No smartphone? Same plan, on a shared computer.

The IHH lobby computer, a library desk, a case manager’s screen turned around. Mouse and keyboard, often a stranger used it last. The plan leaves on paper.

What changes from the phone

Fewer screensLanguage and “say it your way” share one screen. Plan and “take it with you” share another. Two columnsTask on the left, help on the right, each with one left edge. Nothing centers but button labels. Stacks under 1024 px. Print firstThe main action on the plan is PRINT MY PLAN. A text goes only to a number they give staff. Bigger typeBody 20 px, questions 40 px. People sit farther from a monitor. Keyboard worksTab through every control. Enter makes the plan. Esc is Quick exit on a safety screen.
Start over, always A button in the top bar on every screen. It clears the words, the taps and the plan.
Clears itself After 3 quiet minutes, a 30-second warning, then everything is wiped.
Leaves no trace No sign-in, no saved history, no autofill. Nothing is stored on the computer.
Safety is stricter Someone may be standing behind them. No printout on a safety path, and Quick exit is one key.

Screen anatomy · 1280 × 800

  1. Help strip with 9-1-1 and 2-1-1, as on the phone.
  2. Top bar: logo on the left; language and START OVER on the right. Back sits above the question.
  3. Task column: one question, the input, one yellow action.
  4. Help column in sand: how it works, why a door fits, what we never ask.
